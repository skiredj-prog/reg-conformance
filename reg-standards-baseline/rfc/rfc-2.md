# RFC-2: Structural Admissibility & Constraint Geometry

**Status:** Proposed Standard (Draft)
**Dependencies:** RFC-0 (Constitution), RFC-1 (Wire Protocol)

**Purpose:** Defines how the Structure dimension evaluates the real-time admissibility of an action based on the system's structural state. It establishes the normative definitions of the geometric primitives, the strict boundary between measurement and interpretation, and the rules for handling epistemic uncertainty.

> **REG does not govern what the world is. It governs what execution remains admissible given what the system can legitimately establish about the world at the execution boundary.**

---

## Major Decisions Locked

- **Geometry as a Normative Class of Reasoning:** the formula $S = K / (P \times V + \varepsilon)$ is defined as the normative reference model for Structural Admissibility. Alternative mathematical models are permitted for conformance, provided they satisfy the epistemic, invariant, and conservatism constraints defined in this RFC.

- **The Four-Level Epistemic Boundary:** the membrane strictly separates Observation, Measurement, Interpretation, and Decision. It MUST NOT present an interpretation as an observed fact.

- **Conservative Admissibility:** uncertainty MUST be represented as a constraint that contracts the admissible region, not merely as a cosmetic confidence multiplier applied to a final score.

- **Invariant Supremacy:** hard invariants bypass geometric evaluation entirely. Furthermore, the absence of sufficient evidence to establish that an invariant is satisfied MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the invariant is satisfied.

---

## 1. Normative Definitions of the Primitives

The Structure dimension evaluates the compatibility of an action with the current state of the system. While implementations may use various mathematical models, the standard defines three normative primitives that must be explicitly represented:

**Pressure (P):** the intensity of the demand the action exerts on the system. It is not merely the "size" of the action, but the ratio of the action's demand to the system's normal operational bandwidth and contextual limits.

**Volatility / Irreversibility (V):** the measure of the action's blast radius and state mutation depth. It evaluates how far the effect propagates and the structural difficulty of reconstructing the prior state.

**Capacity (K):** the system's current bandwidth to absorb the action. This encompasses operational capacity (system load), epistemic capacity (quality and freshness of available data), and institutional capacity (temporal and policy constraints).

---

## 2. The Epistemic Boundary

To maintain the integrity of the Execution Boundary, the Structure Gate MUST NOT evaluate actions based on assumed, inferred, or hallucinated states. It MUST operate strictly within the bounds of verifiable, provenanced data. The evaluation process is strictly divided into four distinct levels.

### 2.1 Observation

Observation represents the raw facts, signals, or telemetry existing in the environment. Observations are generated by the environment, sensors, or external systems. The REG membrane MUST NOT generate observations. It only consumes them. An observation without provenance is treated as invalid for the purpose of structural evaluation.

### 2.2 Measurement

A Measurement is an observation that has been instrumented, contextualized, and bound to its provenance. Every Measurement used by the Structure Gate MUST carry the following normative properties:

- `value` — the quantified or qualified datum.
- `unit` — the normative scale or category.
- `observed_at` — the exact timestamp the fact occurred in the environment.
- `received_at` — the timestamp the membrane received the datum.
- `source` — the cryptographic or logical identifier of the origin.
- `provenance` — the chain of custody or transformation from raw observation to measurement.
- `freshness` — the acceptable time-to-live (TTL) for this specific datum.
- `confidence` — the epistemic quality of the measurement (see §2.5).

### 2.3 Interpretation

Interpretation is the deterministic transformation of Measurements into structural variables (e.g., P, V, K) and the evaluation of the Admissible Region.

Interpretations MUST be produced by deterministic, versioned mapping functions. An Interpretation MUST NOT contain semantic business logic (which belongs to the proposing agent). It strictly evaluates structural compatibility.

### 2.4 Decision

The Decision is the final mapping of the Interpretation to one of the four REG dispositions (PASS, FLAG, HOLD, HARD_VETO).

The Decision MUST be strictly derived from the Interpretation and the applicable Invariants. The membrane MUST NOT alter a Decision based on external pressures, historical success rates, or non-structural heuristics.

### 2.5 Epistemic States: Unknown, Stale, Missing, and Contradictory

The membrane MUST NOT collapse distinct epistemic failures into a single "low confidence" score. The following states MUST be distinguished and handled explicitly:

**UNKNOWN:** no sufficient basis exists to establish the state.
Rule: absence of evidence to establish that an invariant is satisfied MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the invariant is satisfied. UNKNOWN defaults to HOLD or HARD_VETO, never PASS.

**STALE:** the measurement exists but has exceeded its defined freshness window.
Rule: STALE measurements MUST be excluded from the Interpretation. If the stale measurement was critical for an Invariant, the result is HOLD or HARD_VETO.

**CONTRADICTORY:** multiple valid measurements provide incompatible values for the same structural variable.
Rule: CONTRADICTORY states MUST trigger a HOLD to prevent execution under ambiguous conditions, unless a deterministic, pre-declared resolution policy exists.

**LOW CONFIDENCE:** the measurement is available and fresh, but the source or provenance indicates degraded quality.
Rule: LOW CONFIDENCE MUST trigger Conservative Admissibility (§2.8).

### 2.6 Prohibited Inference

To maintain the integrity of the Execution Boundary, the Structure Gate MUST NOT:

- Infer the internal semantic intent of the proposing agent.
- Guess or interpolate missing measurements to produce a "best estimate" for execution authorization.
- Assume that a system state remains unchanged if no new measurement is received (the "no news is good news" fallacy is strictly prohibited).
- Bypass or downgrade an Invariant check due to computational cost or latency constraints.

### 2.7 Provenance and Reproducibility

A structural verdict is only as valid as its ability to be independently reconstructed.

**reason chain:** the Structure Gate MUST output a reason chain that links the final Decision back to the specific Interpretations, which in turn link to the exact Measurements used. A conforming third party MUST be able to replay the exact same Measurements through the same versioned Interpretation functions and deterministically reproduce the Decision.

### 2.8 Conservative Admissibility (The Treatment of Uncertainty)

Uncertainty in the structural evaluation MUST NOT be handled by applying a cosmetic multiplier to the final score (e.g., Score × 0.73). This creates an uninterpretable black box.

Instead, uncertainty MUST be represented as a constraint that contracts the Admissible Region.

- **Known State:** the Admissible Region is evaluated at its nominal boundaries.
- **Uncertain State (Low Confidence / Partial Measurement):** the membrane MUST evaluate the action against the worst-case plausible bounds of the uncertain variables.
- **Monotonic Conservatism:** as uncertainty increases, the Admissible Region MUST strictly shrink or remain identical. It MUST NEVER expand.

If the action is not admissible under the worst-case plausible bounds, the disposition MUST be degraded (e.g., from PASS to FLAG, or FLAG to HOLD).

## 3. The Admissibility Evaluation Model

While implementations may use alternative mathematical models (as permitted in §1), the standard defines the Constraint Geometry model as the normative reference for evaluating structural admissibility.

### 3.1 The Reference Formula

The reference model calculates the Admissibility Score (S) as follows:

$$S = \frac{K}{P \times V + \varepsilon}$$

- **K (Capacity):** the current structural capacity.
- **P (Pressure):** the demand intensity.
- **V (Volatility):** the irreversibility / blast radius.
- **ε (Epsilon):** a stability floor to prevent division by zero and bound the maximum possible score.

### 3.2 Admissible Regions and Dispositions

The score S is mapped to the REG dispositions based on pre-declared, versioned thresholds:

| Condition | Disposition |
|---|---|
| $S \geq \text{Threshold}_{PASS}$ | PASS (subject to Confidence Modulation) |
| $\text{Threshold}_{FLAG} \leq S < \text{Threshold}_{PASS}$ | FLAG |
| $\text{Threshold}_{HOLD} \leq S < \text{Threshold}_{FLAG}$ | HOLD |
| $S < \text{Threshold}_{HOLD}$ | HARD_VETO |

### 3.3 Invariant Supremacy

Hard invariants (e.g., "never exceed €5,000") are evaluated before geometric scoring.

If a hard invariant is violated, the geometry is bypassed; the disposition is immediately HARD_VETO. Geometry evaluates the residual admissibility only when hard invariants are not immediately violated.

### 3.4 Confidence Modulation & One-Way Hardening

If the epistemic state of the measurements (as defined in §2.5) indicates uncertainty, the Confidence Modulator is applied.

**One-Way Hardening:** the modulator can only degrade a disposition (e.g., PASS → FLAG), never upgrade it. The modulation is achieved by contracting the Admissible Region (§2.8), effectively raising the bar for a PASS when data quality is low.

## 4. Interoperability and Conformance

To ensure REG remains an open standard and does not become a proprietary description of a single implementation, conformance is based on behavioral and epistemic properties, not specific code.

### 4.1 Conformance Criteria for Alternative Models

An alternative mathematical model for the Structure Gate is REG-conformant if and only if it demonstrably satisfies:

- **Invariant Supremacy:** hard invariants always result in HARD_VETO, regardless of the model's internal scoring.
- **Monotonic Conservatism:** uncertainty strictly contracts the admissible region; it never expands it.
- **Epistemic Separation:** the model strictly separates Measurement from Interpretation and prohibits the inferences listed in §2.6.
- **Reproducibility:** the model's transformations are deterministic and versioned.

### 4.2 Test Vectors and Conformance Suite

RFC-7 will define the formal conformance suite. For the Structure dimension, conformance is proven via:

- **Standardized Test Vectors:** a public corpus of input states (Measurements) and expected outputs (Dispositions).
- **Edge Case Validation:** specific tests for UNKNOWN, STALE, and CONTRADICTORY states to prove the implementation correctly defaults to HOLD/HARD_VETO rather than silently passing.
- **Drift Testing:** tests that verify the system correctly invalidates a PASS when the underlying Measurements become STALE.
