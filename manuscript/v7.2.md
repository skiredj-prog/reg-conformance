## Abstract

An admitted operation may produce an external effect while its receipt remains unavailable. Retrying on silence can then duplicate the effect. We study a fail-closed execution membrane that governs attempt admission, single-use permits, evidence-bound reconciliation and quarantine for cross-Realm operations.

The formal argument separates the at-most-once-effect objective from local invariants and states the transition obligations and epistemic assumptions needed to connect them. A concealed effect combined with false non-execution evidence gives a model-specific counterexample; cryptographic integrity alone cannot establish external-world truth.

The empirical material combines 53 fault sequences, bounded explicit-state exploration and targeted conformance scenarios. In the 53-sequence comparison, a reused target-side idempotency key and the membrane prevent duplicate effects under the declared model. Evidence-bound reconciliation additionally establishes the expected operation outcomes under reliable, truthful evidence assumptions. Without reconciliation, the membrane blocks 96 retry attempts across 51 sequences.

Bounded explicit-state exploration under a two-by-two matrix of assumption violations finds an at-most-once violation only when an effect is hidden from the membrane and a false non-execution attestation is accepted. The three-transition witness persists when that attestation carries a valid signature from a trusted key. The other regimes record no violation within depth seven or eight. The results support bounded, revision-specific observations and a conditional safety argument, not an unconditional end-to-end guarantee, general concurrency safety or production certification.

**Keywords:** cross-Realm governance; execution safety; epistemic uncertainty; fail-closed; reconciliation; fault injection; bounded model checking.

## 1. Introduction

Autonomous agents now request actions whose effects materialize in systems they do not control. Between "the action was authorized" and "the outcome is known" lies an interval in which the requester can be wrong about the world. The standard remedy, retrying until an acknowledgement arrives, is safe only if the target deduplicates. When it does not, a lost acknowledgement turns a single decision into two effects.

This paper isolates that interval. We ask a narrow question: given an admitted transition whose outcome the client cannot observe, what must a boundary component guarantee, under which assumptions, and at what cost?

The scope is deliberately small. We do not address model alignment, intra-Realm correctness or the quality of the admissibility score. The score is an input from a reference kernel (§3.2); the contribution is the lifecycle discipline around it.

**Contributions.**

* **Model (§4).** A transition system with explicit guards and eight invariants, written so that every guard corresponds to a named code path.
* **Conditional safety result (§5).** A conditional preservation theorem and a bounded counterexample illustrating how the at-most-once-effect property can fail under joint violation of the observational and attestation assumptions.
* **Bounded explicit-state exploration (§6).** Exploration of the executable membrane over its public API, under a $2 \times 2$ matrix of assumption violations, on the historical and the current membrane revision.
* **Scenario campaign (§7).** Scripted and targeted scenarios S1–S12, with CI evidence for the implemented cases and explicit limits on what each establishes.
* **Adversarial model track (§7.11).** A supplementary finite-state Lab that records expected counterexamples for signature-versus-standing collapse, cross-Realm bypass, transition rebinding, and two-thread concurrent admission. Non-blocking in CI; not a conformance verdict.
* **Baseline comparison (§8).** An exhaustive enumeration of fault sequences, reported with its costs.
* **Disclosure of limits (§10).** Including implementation seams found and resolved in the code.

**Non-claims.** This paper does not claim general safety under concurrency, a distributed lock, the legitimacy of a caller-supplied public key, truthfulness of an attestation about the external world, conformance of any production gateway, or that the approach outperforms alternatives in deployed settings.

This paper evaluates the membrane layer of the TENIR architecture: the component that governs the lifecycle of execution attempts after they have been admitted, and that produces the dispositions `PASS`, `HOLD`, `QUARANTINE_LOCK`, and `FAILED` at the execution boundary.

## 2. Problem Statement, Fault Model and Related Work

### 2.1 Setting

A Realm is an administrative and technical domain with its own constraint geometry. A transition $\tau : R_A \to R_B$ is an action whose effect materializes in $R_B$. A logical operation is identified by a key $l$ (called LEI in the implementation). A client makes one or more attempts $a$ to perform the operation; each attempt has a unique identifier.

Local admissibility does not entail cross-Realm admissibility:

$$
\text{Admissible}(a \mid R_A) \not\Rightarrow \text{Admissible}(\tau : R_A \to R_B)
$$

This is the motivation for adjudicating the transition. The present paper concerns what happens after admission.

### 2.2 Fault model

We consider omission and delay faults on the response path and on the evidence path:

* loss of a request before the target (S1a);
* loss of an acknowledgement after the effect (S1b);
* loss of a rejection (S1c);
* no qualifying receipt within a bound $\tau_K$ (S2);
* a second attempt while the first is unresolved (S3);
* a receipt arriving after the window closed or after its token expired (S4);
* bound evidence items that disagree (S5);
* later evidence that settles an unresolved attempt (S6).

Out of scope: general Byzantine targets, clock skew between processes, and unconstrained concurrent callers. The regimes of §6 that violate $A$ model one Byzantine behaviour only: a false non-execution attestation. S8 adds a targeted two-thread admission-race test; it does not establish general thread safety.

### 2.3 Assumptions

* **$A_1$ (sequential).** Calls to the membrane are totally ordered.
* **$A_2$ (one dispatch).** An attempt dispatches at most once and a dispatch produces at most one external effect.
* **$O$ (observation).** If a dispatch produces an external effect, the membrane records it (`effect_observed`).
* **$A$ (attestation).** An upstream attestation that an operation did not execute is true. Boundary $U_2$ (§4.0) is the reliance on this assumption.
* **$D$ (declarative effect attributes).** The effect attributes declared in a `CrossRealmTransition` (exposure, reversibility, target class) are truthfully declared by the caller. The membrane evaluates the realm policy against those attributes; it does not verify them against the external world. The S12 checks do not establish the truth of those declarations; the archived S12e case concerns a missing target postcondition.

$A_1$ and $A_2$ are structural in the implementation. $O$ and $A$ are the two epistemic assumptions whose roles §5 separates; $D$ bounds the interpretation of the Cross-Realm attribute checks.

### 2.4 Related work

**Reference monitors and PEP/PDP.** Anderson's reference monitor concept and XACML-style PEP/PDP architectures evaluate requests against policy at the time of the request [1, 8]. Schneider's characterization of enforceable policies concerns safety properties that a monitor can enforce from observed execution [2]; our setting adds the case in which part of the execution is unobservable to the requester.

**Retries, idempotency and sagas.** Idempotency keys let a target absorb duplicates; sagas compensate partial progress [3]; Helland argues for designing around at-least-once delivery [4]. These techniques are effective when the target cooperates. The end-to-end argument [9] explains why: duplicate suppression is complete only where the effect is applied. §8 shows that a PEP/PDP with a reused idempotency key achieves zero duplicates on our metric, and positions the membrane relative to it.

**Impossibility of common knowledge over lossy links.** The two-generals problem [5] explains why no protocol over a lossy channel can give both parties certainty about the other's state. The membrane does not escape this; it chooses what to do while uncertain (hold), and when uncertainty may be resolved (only on bound, fresh, qualified evidence). Two-phase commit has the same blind spot: a participant that has voted to commit and lost the coordinator is blocked in an uncertainty period [10]. `HOLD` plays the role of that blocked state.

**Formal methods.** Explicit-state exploration of a specification [6, 7] is standard. Systematic exploration of implementation code through its interface goes back to VeriSoft [11] and CMC [12]. Our exploration applies to the executable implementation through its public API rather than to a separate specification.

**Companion work.** The REG Core Protocol working paper (record: `https://zenodo.org/records/23252697`, version v0.3.0 v3) addresses the protocol layer; this manuscript does not rely on its content beyond the shared vocabulary. The membrane evaluated here is in `tenir-conformance-s1-g1`; the scoring kernel is `tenir_governance`.

## 4. Governance Framework and the Autonomous Unit (TAU)

This section establishes the functional governance contract for a logical operation and identifies the Autonomous Unit (TAU) as the decision-making unit capable of acting, consistent with Blueprint v4.4 §0.1. It distinguishes that functional contract from the technical invariants governing the membrane's local state transitions.

The TAU operates within a declared scope and under the applicable governance constraints, evidence requirements, and authorized resolution mechanisms. It does not possess unlimited authority over external systems, determine external truth by declaration, or guarantee that every real-world effect is observable. In particular, the absence of a receipt is not, by itself, evidence that an operation did not execute.

Throughout this section and the remainder of the manuscript, TAU means Autonomous Unit. The expressions "Tenant Authority Unit," "Threshold of Autonomous Unity," and "Temporal Admissibility Unit" are not used.

The governance contract distinguishes three outcomes relevant to execution control. `HOLD` is a disposition that withholds admission pending sufficient evidence or review. `QUARANTINE_LOCK` is a persistent LEI-level guard that blocks new admission and dispatch until an authorized resolution. Neither term changes the truth of external events, and neither permits the membrane to infer non-execution from silence.

### 4.0 Functional Declaration: The Autonomous Unit (TAU)

#### 1. Functional definition and scope

The Autonomous Unit (TAU) is the decision-making unit capable of acting, as defined in Blueprint v4.4 §0.1. For the purposes of this manuscript, it governs the admission and lifecycle of attempts associated with a logical operation identified by a Logical Execution Identifier (LEI), within a declared governance scope.

The TAU may admit an attempt, refuse admission, place an operation on `HOLD`, or engage a `QUARANTINE_LOCK`, subject to the applicable contract and authorization constraints. These powers are bounded: the TAU governs the membrane's admissibility decisions but does not control external-world truth, make an unobserved effect disappear, or independently establish that a remote operation did not execute.

Attempt-level state and LEI-level governance state are distinct. An attempt may reach a terminal status such as `RESOLVED` or `FAILED`. A `QUARANTINE_LOCK` applies at the LEI level and prevents new execution admission; it does not rewrite the terminal status of an existing attempt.

#### 2. Preservation objective: safety takes priority over liveness

The functional safety objective is at-most-once effect. For each LEI $L$ and every finite execution-history prefix $\pi$, let $E_L(\pi)$ denote the number of externally effective outcomes attributable to $L$ in that prefix. The invariant is:

$$
I_1(\pi): |E_L(\pi)| \le 1
$$

The property concerns real external effects, not merely effects recorded in the membrane's local state. Counting an effect only when it is observed would weaken the property and would fail to detect a concealed outcome.

Safety takes priority over liveness. If the evidence does not establish that a further attempt is safe under the declared assumptions and transition rules, the system must withhold admission. A timeout, network partition, missing receipt, or unacknowledged dispatch does not by itself establish non-execution.

The at-most-once property is a safety property: a violation can be demonstrated by a finite execution prefix containing more than one externally effective outcome for the same LEI. The property does not itself guarantee that an operation eventually completes.

#### 3. Epistemic boundaries: $U_1$, $U_2$, and $U_3$

The TAU receives information about external execution through evidence available to the membrane. Cryptographic integrity and source authentication can support provenance and tamper detection; they do not, by themselves, establish the physical truth of an attestation.

* **$U_1$ — Ambiguity of silence.** A timeout, network partition, lost response, or unacknowledged dispatch leaves execution status unknown unless separate qualifying evidence establishes the outcome. Silence must not be treated as proof of non-execution. It cannot, by itself, justify `FAILED` or the admission of a subsequent attempt.
* **$U_2$ — Attestation reliance.** The reasoning that relies on a non-execution attestation depends on assumption $A$ in §2.3: the relevant attestation is truthful. A cryptographically authenticated statement can still be false about the external world. Authentication and integrity do not entail truthfulness.
* **$U_3$ — The concealment limitation.** $U_3$ names a specific limitation of the end-to-end safety argument. The threat involves an authenticated but false non-execution attestation. The historical model represents it as a qualified declaration; the current replay (§6.2) exercises it with an Ed25519 attestation that the membrane verifies. A witness to this limitation in the model requires the following conditions:
  1. An attempt produces a real external effect that the membrane does not observe or register before a later admission decision.
  2. A false non-execution attestation (a qualified declaration in the historical model; a verified signed attestation in the current replay) permits that attempt to be declared `FAILED`, or otherwise causes the admission guard to accept a subsequent attempt.
  3. The subsequent attempt is dispatched and produces another real effect for the same LEI.

This sequence can violate $I_1$. The general failure of assumption $A$, denoted $\neg A$, is not identical to $U_3$. A false attestation does not necessarily produce an $I_1$ violation if observation or another effective guard prevents the subsequent dispatch. $U_3$ identifies the specific concealment conditions under which the safety argument fails.

#### 4. Admissibility and failure behavior

**Evidentiary prerequisite.** A subsequent attempt $A_n$ may be admitted only if every prior attempt associated with the LEI satisfies the applicable terminal-state and evidence requirements. A recorded effect count of zero is not sufficient when it counts only observed effects and observation completeness is unavailable.

**Contradictory or unbound evidence.** Contradictory evidence, expired or improperly bound evidence, and evidence that fails the applicable integrity or authority checks must not be resolved through optimistic inference. Where the conflict prevents a safe admission decision, execution remains blocked pending qualifying evidence or authorized resolution.

**`HOLD`.** `HOLD` withholds admission for the decision at issue while the evidence or governance question remains unresolved. It is a disposition, not automatically a persistent LEI-level quarantine. A later decision must be based on admissible evidence and the applicable transition rules.

**`QUARANTINE_LOCK`.** `QUARANTINE_LOCK` blocks new attempt admission and execution dispatch for the LEI until an authorized release or resolution transition succeeds. It does not erase or reopen terminal attempt records. Read-only inspection, append-only evidence recording, and explicitly authorized reconciliation may remain available, provided those transitions cannot dispatch an operation or bypass the lock. Release must be authenticated and authorized under the applicable governance contract.

When the system cannot establish that a subsequent execution is safe, it must withhold admission. Whether the appropriate disposition is `HOLD` or `QUARANTINE_LOCK` depends on the declared transition and escalation rules; the two states must not be treated as interchangeable.

### 4.1 State

For each attempt $a$ on operation $l$:

* $\sigma(a) \in \{\text{PENDING}, \text{AWAITING}, \text{RESOLVED}, \text{FAILED}, \text{UNKNOWN}, \text{ESCALATED}\}$;
* $e(a) \in \{0, 1\}$: the membrane has recorded an external effect for $a$;
* $q(a) \in \{0, 1\}$: the outcome is qualified;
* a permit $p(a) \in \{\text{NONE}, \text{ISSUED}, \text{CONSUMED}, \text{REVOKED}\}$ and a nonce.

Globally: a quarantine set $Q$ of operations, an evidence registry, and the effect sink. Let $w(a)$ denote the world effect count of $a$, the number of external effects actually produced by $a$. The membrane cannot read $w$; it records $e$. Assumption $O$ states $w(a) > 0 \Rightarrow e(a) = 1$.

### 4.2 Client projection

The client sees only $\pi_{\text{client}}(a) \in \{\text{RESOLVED}, \text{FAILED}, (\text{UNKNOWN}, \text{HOLD})\}$. In the implementation, S1a, S1b and S1c all project to $(\text{UNKNOWN}, \text{HOLD})$, although $w(a)$ is $0$, $1$ and $0$. This is client-side projection indiscernibility. The membrane's internal $e(a)$ differs across the three cases; the client's view does not.

### 4.3 Admission guard

A new attempt $a'$ on $l$ is admitted iff:

$$
G_{\text{adm}}(l, a') \iff a' \notin \text{dom}(\sigma) \wedge l \notin Q \wedge \forall a \text{ on } l : \big(\sigma(a) = \text{FAILED} \wedge \text{NEA}(a)\big)
$$

where $\text{NEA}(a)$ (non-execution attested) is a predicate on the membrane's own state. It holds when the attempt reached `FAILED` through an observed rejection, a kernel veto before dispatch, or an attestation accepted under the `non_execution_attested` rule of §4.4; $e(a) = 0$ alone does not satisfy it. The membrane can evaluate $\text{NEA}$. Whether non-execution is true of the external world is not a state predicate: it is assumption $A$.

On admission a permit is issued. If the kernel vetoes, the permit is not consumed. Otherwise it is consumed at dispatch.

| `dispatch, request lost` | `new` | $G_{\text{adm}}$, kernel admissible | `UNKNOWN`, $e := 0$ |
| `dispatch, rejection observed` | `new` | $G_{\text{adm}}$, kernel admissible | `FAILED`, $e := 0$, qualified |

`reconcile` never invokes the kernel or the sink and never issues a permit. It does not reopen an expired receipt window, does not consume or re-issue an execution permit, and does not appear as an effect-producing transition under $I_4$.

`non_execution_attested` (normative name). An attempt may be marked `non_execution_attested` only when all of the following hold: (i) the attempt is in a non-terminal or `UNKNOWN` path eligible for T7 / reconcile-`FAILED`; (ii) qualifying evidence is bound to that attempt (`id`, `LEI`, `nonce`) and is fresh under the membrane's evidence rules; (iii) the observed effect count for the attempt is $e = 0$; and (iv) a signed non-execution attestation is accepted under the path's verification rules (lab field: `non_execution_confirmed`). The mark authorizes the transition to terminal `FAILED` without dispatch. It does not establish that the attestation is true of the external world, nor that the caller-supplied verification key is legitimate; those remain assumptions $A$ and the trust-root obligation in S7/D11. Under $I_7$, subsequent admission may rely on `FAILED` only when non-execution has been established by admissible evidence in this sense — not by silence alone.

`retry_eligible` is an audit annotation only. It is not a guard in $G_{\text{adm}}$ and does not authorize dispatch, reconcile, or admission. This holds at the evaluated revision `d42499b`. The later revision `8bc0e5e` adds it as a further admission condition; the E1 results and the three-transition witness reproduce on that revision, the E2 matrix was not rerun on it.

### 4.5 Preamble

To connect the functional preservation objective declared by the Autonomous Unit with the membrane's operational state machine, we specify the following technical invariants. These invariants constrain local transitions; their ability to establish a property about real external effects additionally depends on the environmental assumptions and on the precise definition of effect-producing transitions.

The derivation below distinguishes an invariant itself from the transition conditions required to use that invariant in a proof. In particular, the absence of an observed effect is not equivalent to the absence of a real effect when observation completeness does not hold.

### 4.5a At-Most-Once Effect and Supporting Invariants $I_1$–$I_8$

**Scope and notation.** Let $\pi$ denote a finite execution-history prefix of the modeled transition system, and let $E_L(\pi)$ be the number of real external effects for LEI $L$ along that prefix. $I_1$ is a property of every valid execution-history prefix, not merely of a final snapshot:

$$
I_1(\pi): |E_L(\pi)| \le 1
$$

The invariants below describe the obligations required to preserve $I_1$. They do not establish the theorem merely by being listed. The proof also requires that the transition relation implement the stated dispatch, evidence, and admission conditions.

| Invariant | Formal rule / property | Role in the preservation argument and required qualification |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **$I_1$ — At-most-once effect** | $\lvert E_L(\pi)\rvert \le 1$ for every valid finite execution-history prefix $\pi$ | The functional safety property to be preserved. The counter counts real external effects, not only effects observed or recorded locally. |
| **$I_2$ — Permit single-use** | A permit bound to an attempt and LEI may authorize at most one effect-producing dispatch and cannot be consumed again. | Prevents replay of the same permit. The transition rules must ensure that every effect-producing dispatch requires the appropriate attempt-bound permit and that the permit cannot authorize repeated dispatches. |
| **$I_3$ — Attempt terminality and immutability** | Once an attempt reaches `RESOLVED` or `FAILED`, its terminal status cannot be changed or reopened. | Prevents retroactive rewriting of an attempt's outcome. This is distinct from the LEI-level `QUARANTINE_LOCK`. Append-only evidence and reconciliation records may be permitted, but they must not silently rewrite terminal attempt status or re-enable dispatch. |
| **$I_4$ — Only dispatch produces effects** | A real effect can be produced only by a valid effect-producing dispatch under the modeled transition rules. Each such dispatch produces at most one real effect for the modeled operation. | Excludes effect-producing transitions that bypass dispatch. The at-most-one-effect-per-dispatch condition must be explicit in the model; it does not follow merely from naming a transition "dispatch." |
| **$I_5$ — `FAILED` implies no observed effect** | $\sigma(A_k) = \text{FAILED} \Rightarrow e_{\text{obs}}(A_k) = 0$ | This is a statement about observed effects. By itself it does not establish that the real effect count is zero. Under $O$, a real effect cannot remain unobserved within the scope of $O$. Alternatively, a truthful non-execution attestation under $A$ can establish non-execution for the attempt it covers. |
| **$I_6$ — No admission under quarantine** | $l \in Q \Rightarrow \neg G_{\text{adm}}(l, a')$ | Blocks attempt creation for execution, permit-based authorization of a new attempt, and effect-producing dispatch while the lock is active. Read-only inspection, append-only evidence updates, permitted reconciliation, and authorized release may remain available. |
| **$I_7$ — Admission condition** | $\text{Admitted}(A_n) \Rightarrow \forall k < n : \sigma(A_k) = \text{FAILED} \wedge \text{NEA}(A_k)$ | Requires every prior attempt to be terminally failed and requires evidence sufficient to establish non-execution under the applicable assumptions. A zero count of observed effects alone is not sufficient if $O$ is absent and no truthful attestation under $A$ establishes non-execution. |
| **$I_8$ — Attempt-history permanence** | For valid prefixes $\pi_1 \sqsubseteq \pi_2$, the attempt records in $\pi_1$ remain present and unaltered in $\pi_2$. | Prevents deletion, reuse, or retroactive rewriting of attempt history. New evidence may be appended under the evidence rules, but existing attempt records cannot be pruned to bypass $I_7$. |

Here, $e_{\text{obs}}$ denotes the observed effect count for an attempt. $\text{NEA}$ is the state predicate of §4.3. It is not defined by $e_{\text{obs}} = 0$, and it establishes real non-execution only under $A$.

**Transition-level proof obligations.** To use $I_2$–$I_8$ to establish $I_1$, the modeled transition relation must additionally satisfy all of the following:

1. The initial state has no real effects for the LEI.
2. Every transition that increases the real effect count is an effect-producing dispatch covered by $I_4$.
3. Each attempt's effect-producing dispatch is protected by its unique single-use permit under $I_2$, with no alternative dispatch path.
4. Under $O$, every real effect in scope is represented in the observation/evidence state before a subsequent admission decision can rely on its absence.
5. A transition to `FAILED` cannot be based solely on timeout, silence, or missing observation. It requires evidence admissible under the declared contract.
6. Under $A$, an attestation of non-execution used for admission is truthful for the attempt and operation it covers.
7. $I_7$ checks all prior attempts, and $I_8$ prevents prior attempts from being omitted or rewritten. $I_6$ blocks subsequent admission while the LEI-level quarantine lock is active.

Assumptions $A_1$ and $A_2$ remain exactly as defined in §2.3. Their required role in the transition relation must be stated where used; the invariant labels alone do not establish their operational satisfaction.

## 5. Conditional Preservation of the Functional Contract

The formal relationship between the functional preservation objective and the technical invariants is expressed as a conditional preservation claim. The claim concerns the real external effect count across all finite execution-history prefixes of the modeled transition system. Its validity depends on the transition-level obligations in §4.5a and on the applicable observational and attestation assumptions, $O$ and $A$, as defined in §2.3.

The following result must therefore be read as a conditional safety argument for the specified model, not as an unconditional guarantee for arbitrary external systems or for an implementation whose transitions have not been shown to refine that model.

### 5.1 Preservation claim

**Theorem 1 — Conditional preservation of at-most-once effect.**  
Let $S_0$ be the initial state of the modeled membrane transition system. Let $\pi$ be any finite execution-history prefix reachable from $S_0$, and let $E_L(\pi)$ denote the real external effects attributable to LEI $L$ in that prefix.

If the following conditions hold:

1. the initial state contains zero real external effects for $L$;
2. invariants $I_2$–$I_8$ hold on every transition along $\pi$;
3. every real effect is produced only by a valid effect-producing dispatch, and each such dispatch produces at most one real effect for its attempt;
4. every effect-producing dispatch requires the unique single-use permit bound to that attempt and LEI;
5. $O$ holds for the effect-observation scope used by the admission decision, or the relevant non-execution conclusion is independently established by a truthful attestation under $A$;
6. every prior attempt must satisfy $I_7$ before a subsequent attempt can be admitted, with `FAILED` supported by admissible evidence of non-execution rather than silence alone;
7. no transition outside the specified dispatch rules can increase the real external effect count;

then:

$$
|E_L(\pi)| \le 1
$$

for every such reachable finite execution-history prefix $\pi$.

This is a conditional theorem. Its hypotheses must be checked against the actual transition relation. A successful bounded exploration is evidence about the explored model and its tested implementation; it does not discharge the general proof obligations by itself. Conditions 2–4, 6 and 7 restate the supporting invariants and transition obligations, so the theorem is a reduction: it shows that these obligations suffice for $I_1$ under $O$ or $A$. It does not show that the implemented transition relation meets them; that step is checked only empirically, within the bounds of §6.

### 5.2 Proof structure

* **Base case.** In the initial state $S_0$, the real external effect count for $L$ is zero by condition 1. Therefore $|E_L(\pi_0)| = 0 \le 1$.
* **Inductive hypothesis.** Assume that the property holds for a reachable prefix $\pi$: $|E_L(\pi)| \le 1$.
* **Inductive step.** Consider a valid transition $t$ extending $\pi$ to $\pi'$.
  * If $t$ is not an effect-producing dispatch, condition 7 ensures that it does not increase the real effect count. Therefore the bound is preserved.
  * If $t$ is an effect-producing dispatch and the prior real effect count is zero, condition 3 permits at most one new real effect. Thus the count in $\pi'$ is at most one.
  * If a real effect has already occurred, a further effect-producing dispatch must be prevented. $I_2$ prevents replay of the consumed permit for the same attempt. $I_7$ prevents a subsequent attempt from being admitted until the preceding attempts satisfy the terminal and non-execution evidence requirements. Under $O$, an earlier real effect cannot remain unobserved within the scope of the admission decision. Under $A$, a non-execution attestation used to qualify an earlier attempt as `FAILED` cannot truthfully certify non-execution if that attempt produced the effect it covers. Thus, subject to conditions 3–6, a previously effect-producing attempt cannot be used to justify a new attempt on the basis of false or insufficient non-execution evidence.

Consequently, no valid transition increases the real effect count from one to two. The property is preserved at $\pi'$. By induction over finite execution-history prefixes, $I_1$ holds for every reachable prefix satisfying the stated conditions.

**Proof limitation.** This proof is valid only if the listed conditions are enforced by the actual transition relation. In particular, $I_5$ in its observed-effect form is not sufficient on its own to establish the absence of a real effect, and $I_2$–$I_8$ without the dispatch and evidence obligations do not automatically imply $I_1$.

### 5.3 Joint role of $I_2$–$I_8$

The supporting invariants address different ways a later effect could be produced:

* $I_2$ prevents reuse of a consumed attempt-bound permit.
* $I_3$ prevents terminal attempt outcomes from being rewritten or reopened. It does not turn `QUARANTINE_LOCK` into an attempt status.
* $I_4$ restricts effect production to dispatch transitions and must explicitly bound the number of effects produced by each dispatch.
* $I_5$ constrains observed state. The implication from no observed effect to no real effect requires $O$ or an applicable truthful non-execution attestation under $A$.
* $I_6$ blocks admission and dispatch while the LEI is under `QUARANTINE_LOCK`; evidence collection and authorized reconciliation may still proceed under their own transition rules.
* $I_7$ requires all earlier attempts to be terminally failed with non-execution established by admissible evidence before a subsequent attempt is admitted.
* $I_8$ prevents deletion or retroactive rewriting of the history on which $I_7$ relies.

These invariants form a joint preservation argument only under the conditions above. The wording "$I_2$–$I_8$ imply $I_1$" must not be interpreted as an unconditional logical entailment independent of the transition relation or environmental assumptions.

### 5.4 Partial preservation when assumptions do not all hold

**Proposition 2 — Conditional guarantee when $O$ is absent but $A$ holds.**  
Suppose $O$ does not hold. $I_1$ may nevertheless be preserved along execution histories satisfying the following path restriction: every transition to `FAILED` that is used to admit a subsequent attempt is supported by a truthful non-execution attestation under $A$; the attestation covers the relevant attempt and operation; every earlier attempt is checked by $I_7$; and $I_2$–$I_4$, $I_6$, $I_8$, and the applicable transition obligations remain in force.

Under these conditions, the truthful non-execution evidence can establish that the attempts admitted for retry produced no real effect, even though observation completeness is absent. The preservation argument applies only to paths satisfying the restriction.

A path that reaches `FAILED` on the basis of an unobserved rejection, timeout, lost receipt, or other condition that does not establish non-execution is outside this proposition. Where $O$ is absent and no truthful, qualifying attestation establishes non-execution, $I_7$ cannot safely admit a new attempt merely because the observed effect count is zero.

**Proposition 3 — Model-specific counterexample under the concealment boundary.**  
The absence of $O$ and $A$ does not logically force every possible system to violate $I_1$. However, bounded E2 exploration of the adversarial world reaches a finite trace that violates $I_1$, both on the historical membrane revision and on the current revision with a signed attestation (§6.2):

1. `admit_await(A1, HIDDEN_effect)`
2. `declare_failed(A1, qualified=True)`
3. `process(A2, ok)`

In the modeled trace, the first attempt produces a real effect not observed by the membrane. The subsequent qualified failure is accepted without a truthful non-execution basis, and the second attempt produces another real effect for the same LEI. The model's real-world counter reaches two, violating $I_1$.

This trace is a counterexample to the universal $I_1$ claim for the modeled transition system if the listed transitions are admitted under the stated adversarial conditions. The explorer reports this three-transition witness as the shortest $I_1$ violation on both revisions; that empirical result does not establish that every environment violating $O$ or $A$ exhibits the same trace, nor does it demonstrate that all real implementations admit the same transitions.

The trace illustrates $U_3$ because it combines a hidden first effect, a false non-execution attestation, and a subsequent effect-producing dispatch. On the current revision, step 2 submits an Ed25519 attestation that is bound to the attempt, LEI and nonce and signed by a key active in the lab trust root. The membrane verifies it, marks the attempt `FAILED` and unlocks the LEI; the second dispatch then resolves. Signature validity and binding therefore do not establish non-execution: the violation comes from false content, not from a verification failure. The experiment does not test signature forgery or key compromise. In the `false_attest` world, where $O$ holds but $A$ is violated, the same signed attestation is rejected because the membrane has observed the effect (`T7_REJECTED_EFFECT_ALREADY_OBSERVED`); no $I_1$ violation is recorded within depth 6 on the historical revision or depth 7 on the current one. That finding is bounded evidence, not a universal guarantee.

### 5.5 Boundary statement

The Autonomous Unit declares the functional governance contract in §4.0. The technical invariants in §4.5a define the local preservation obligations. The preservation claim holds only where the stated transition and environmental conditions hold.

Under $U_3$, the specified concealment trace demonstrates that local admissibility decisions can fail to preserve the end-to-end at-most-once-effect objective when a real effect is hidden and false non-execution evidence permits a retry. The architecture therefore states this boundary explicitly instead of presenting the conditional result as an unconditional safety guarantee.

> "The functional declaration holds where the stated assumptions and transition obligations hold. Where the evidence cannot establish non-execution, or where the environment can conceal an effect while supplying false non-execution evidence, the architecture declares the boundary rather than claiming a guarantee it cannot support."

### 6.4 Evidence ledger and anchored verification

The membrane mirrors its evidence registry in a hash-chained ledger. Each recorded attestation, verified or rejected, is appended as a deep copy. `verify_evidence_registry` checks the chain and that the registry still matches the ledger record by record: an edit, insertion or deletion is detected at the first affected position, and an edit to the registry alone is caught by the cross-check.

The chain alone does not detect truncation of the tail, since a shorter chain is internally valid. Truncation is detected only against a head published outside the ledger. A checkpoint writes the private entries and a public anchor; a broken ledger is refused before any file is written. At the time of writing, no checkpoint has been produced by a running membrane, so tail truncation is not detected in practice.

### 7.3 S4 and S6 are different mechanisms

S4 rejects a receipt that attempts to qualify an execution after the window closed. S6 uses a separate `reconcile` operation to observe a past outcome. If reconciliation reused the late-receipt path it would become a bypass of S4. The implementation keeps them apart: `reconcile` does not reopen the window, does not call the sink, does not issue permits and does not reuse the first attempt's permit.

### 7.4 The attestation and trust-anchor limit

`non_execution_confirmed` is a declarative upstream attestation. S7 verifies Ed25519 signatures using the public key supplied by the caller; signature verification therefore does not establish the key's legitimacy (D11, issue #14). Nor does a valid signature establish that the attestation is true about the external world. A production deployment must resolve keys through a versioned trust root and bind the attestation to a signed target receipt or an independent audit path. Together with $O$ and $A$, this is the assumption on which §5 rests.

**Decision (trust anchor, laboratory scope):** the trust root is a laboratory fixture supplied by the caller. A signed, pre-registered and versioned key registry is an open next milestone, to be tracked as a separate issue. Key revocation is not implemented.

### 7.5 What the campaign does not show

The S1–S6 scenarios are scripted and sequential, and were written by the same team that wrote the membrane. S7–S12 add signature verification, a targeted concurrent admission race, signed TAU scope checks, governed Transition/commit binding, and cross-Realm verdict tests. These are bounded tests of specified cases, not a general concurrency proof or production REG certification; §6 reduces, but does not remove, the dependence on case selection.

### 7.11 Hypothesis Lab: adversarial models and bounded claims

The Hypothesis Lab is a supplementary finite-state explorer. It is configured as a non-blocking CI step (`continue-on-error: true`). Its result is evidence about the declared model and its expected counterexample traces, not a conformance verdict for the membrane and not a production claim.

For each adversarial model, the intended semantics is inverted from a conventional test suite:

* `FAIL` means the model reached the declared violation. That is the expected outcome for the adversarial variant.
* `PASS` means no violation was reached in that model variant and action set. It does not establish that the implementation enforces the same property on every path.
* `VACUOUS` means the property was not meaningfully exercised by the selected model and actions.
* `ANOMALY` means an expected counterexample failed to appear. The disappearance of an expected violation is not treated as an improvement; it is treated as a divergence from the frozen reference report.

**Claim ledger.** Each Lab entry links a model claim to a counterexample trace and, where applicable, to the pytest path that covers the corresponding implementation behavior. The ledger is a traceability aid, not a one-to-one semantic equivalence proof between the finite model and the implementation.

**Scope of the Lab.** The Lab's claims are bounded to the models it declares, the actions it exposes, and the tested harness paths. The S7b/c and S10a–c tests cover unknown or revoked keys and specific out-of-scope TAU requests. They do not establish a generic standing service. S8 P1–P3 concern the tested harness and the modeled two-thread scenarios only. Multi-process behavior, crash recovery, unconstrained asynchronous interleavings, generic production PKI or standing services, and production REG conformance remain outside scope.

**Aggregate counts are version-specific.** The `7/5/1/0` and `5/3/1/0` figures refer to specific commits and reports. They are not universal suite totals and must be read alongside the commit and report they were produced against.

## 8. Comparison with Baselines

### 8.1 Design

A single logical operation, a fault alphabet per attempt (F0 success; F1 request lost; F2 effect applied, acknowledgement lost; F3 rejection lost; F4 rejection observed), and a client that retries after a no-response outcome (F1–F3), up to three attempts. All sequences consistent with this policy are enumerated: 53 sequences. This is an enumeration, not a probability estimate; sequences are unweighted.

Systems:

* **B1 — stateless PEP/PDP:** kernel decision, then dispatch;
* **B2 — B1 with a target-side idempotency key** reused across retries;
* **M0 — the membrane without any reconciliation;**
* **M1 — the membrane with evidence-bound reconciliation** using truthful attestations between a no-response outcome and the next retry.

### 8.2 Results

The results below retain the original outcome counts and add two trace-conditioned metrics. The 53 enumerated sequences divide into 15 sequences in which no effect is expected and 38 in which an effect is expected.

A correct no-effect outcome requires both zero final effects and positive operation-level evidence establishing non-execution. An unresolved `UNKNOWN` or `HOLD` state alone does not qualify.

A correct single-effect outcome requires exactly one final effect and client-confirmed success. The denominators are determined from the enumerated fault traces, not from the system's outputs.

| System | Duplicate effects | Exactly one effect | No effect | Client-confirmed success | Correct no-effect | Correct single-effect |
|----------------------------------------|---------:|-------:|------:|----------------:|-------------|---------------|
| **B1** Stateless PEP/PDP | 14 | 24 | 15 | 13 | $1/15$ ($6.67\%$) | $7/38$ ($18.42\%$) |
| **B2** PEP/PDP + reused idempotency key | 0 | 38 | 15 | 13 | $1/15$ ($6.67\%$) | $13/38$ ($34.21\%$) |
| **M0** Membrane without reconciliation | 0 | 18 | 35 | 1 | $1/15$ ($6.67\%$) | $1/38$ ($2.63\%$) |
| **M1** Membrane with evidence-bound reconciliation | 0 | 38 | 15 | 38 | $15/15$ ($100\%$) | $38/38$ ($100\%$) |

The correct no-effect metric is deliberately stricter than simply counting sequences with zero effects. It measures whether non-execution is established for the logical operation, rather than merely remaining possible.

These are finite, unweighted model results. They are not estimates of real-world probabilities and do not establish production performance.

**Membrane revision.** The E1 outputs were produced at commit `d19400b`, before the membrane required signed non-execution attestations. Run unchanged against the current revision, the script's M1 path supplies unsigned evidence that the membrane rejects, and M1 degenerates toward M0 (18 single-effect outcomes, 64 blocked retries). With each negative evidence item carrying a signed attestation bound to its attempt, the script reproduces the committed output byte for byte on `d42499b` and on the later revision `8bc0e5e`. The results above therefore hold for the current membrane under the stated evidence assumptions.

### 8.3 Interpretation

**Duplicates.** B1 produces 14 duplicate-effect sequences. B2 and the membrane produce none under the specified model. A reused target-side idempotency key is sufficient to prevent duplicates on this metric; the membrane's contribution is not that idempotency is ineffective, but that execution-lifecycle controls are enforced at the boundary without relying on target-side deduplication.

**Correct single-effect outcomes.** B1 achieves 7 correct single-effect outcomes among 38 effect-expected traces. B2 achieves $13/38$: the reused idempotency key prevents duplicate effects, but does not establish success to the client whenever a receipt is lost. M0 achieves $1/38$ because fail-closed admission blocks later attempts without a reconciliation path. M1 achieves $38/38$ under the modeled assumption that the evidence-bound reconciliation channel is reliable and truthful. The harness derives M1's reconciliation evidence from the ground-truth fault trace, so $15/15$ and $38/38$ are what a perfect evidence channel yields: an upper bound, not a measured rate. A baseline adding a status query to B2 would isolate the membrane's own contribution; it is not part of this comparison.

**Correct no-effect outcomes.** For B1, B2 and M0, only 1 of the 15 no-effect-expected traces establishes non-execution for the entire logical operation. The only no-effect trace correctly resolved by B1, B2 and M0 is the single-attempt F4 trace: the target receives the request, explicitly rejects it, and the client observes the rejection. M1 establishes it for all 15 through the modeled reconciliation mechanism. This result depends on the evidence assumptions; it does not establish that an arbitrary upstream attestation is truthful.

**Availability and blocked attempts.** M0 records 96 blocked retry attempts across 51 of the 53 sequences. These are different quantities: 51 is the number of sequences containing at least one block, while 96 is the total number of blocked attempts. The distinction matters because the client continues its bounded retry loop after an admission refusal treated as `HOLD`.

**Client knowledge.** M1's improved outcome is conditional on reliable, truthful reconciliation evidence. The difference between B2 and M1 therefore reflects not just execution deduplication but the ability to establish an operation's outcome. This is a modeled property of the combined evidence and membrane design, not a demonstrated property of the membrane alone in production.

**Baseline scope.** B1 and B2 do not model stale or contradictory evidence (S4 and S5). The comparison is limited to the declared fault alphabet, one logical operation, at most three attempts, and the specified client retry policy. The sequences are exhaustively enumerated under that policy and are not probability-weighted.

## 9. Separate Track: Multi-TAU Composition Simulator

The RFC-3 §6 simulator in `reg-conformance/composition` is a reference implementation of arbitration among multiple autonomous units that share resources, an invariant or a decision window. It has 17 unit tests, all passing (run locally and in CI). It covers priority enforcement, serialization, Pareto restoration, escalation and fail-closed fallback, and boundary values ($\ge$ for the group LCR threshold, $\le$ for the shared notional cap).

Its limits: the group LCR is an unweighted arithmetic mean; $\tau_K$ is a supplied input; progressive unblocking is a placeholder; output is `print()`-based rather than a structured audit log; tests are deterministic unit scenarios.

This track is independent of §§6–8. Its results are not evidence for the membrane, and the membrane's results are not evidence for composition. They address composition-level arbitration and execution-lifecycle governance respectively.

## 10. Limitations and Threats to Validity

### 10.1 Seams found in the implementation

Two previously reproduced seams were closed in commit `3ef1d45` (membrane fix). One remains open in the pinned conformance harness.

* **Closed — nonce binding and reconciliation entry point.** The `process_transaction` path now requires a non-empty nonce and records it with the attempt, supporting evidence-bound reconciliation through that entry point. Regression tests were added in the same commit.
* **Closed — pre-execution `HARD_VETO` lifecycle.** A kernel veto now places the attempt in `FAILED` with `non_execution_confirmed=True`, records the veto, and unlocks the LEI without consuming an execution permit. This addresses the earlier behavior in which a veto left the attempt unresolved and blocked subsequent legitimate requests.
* **Open in pinned harness — `ESCALATED` remains absorbing.** Following a contradiction on a non-terminal attempt, `reconcile` returns a bad-state response and no authorized exit transition is currently available in the pinned `tenir-conformance-s1-g1` artifact. Terminal-state quarantine has a release procedure, but the non-terminal `ESCALATED` path does not. This remains an operational and availability limitation in the evaluated harness. *(Companion middleware note: `tenir_governance` v5.4.1 contains `HoldManager.authorize_exit()` in `tenir_governance/operations/hold.py`, which records caller-supplied authorization fields in an `ExitReceipt`. The inspected method does not verify a cryptographic signature or independently establish the caller's authority. Separate modules implement $\tau^{-1}$ and EVP assessment. These code observations neither close the pinned harness limitation nor establish production authorization security.)*

The closures above apply to the lab artifact and the tested code revision. They do not establish correctness under concurrency, production evidence authentication, or conformance of a deployed gateway.

* **`PAYLOAD_DIVERGENT`.** A separate terminal observation state is intended for an effect whose payload diverges from the declared one. Its operational effects (quarantine, retry) are undefined, and the state is not implemented.
* **`CT-R4-005` (state at verdict).** The implemented check binds the transition and payload hashes at verdict and at commit. Drift of the external environment between verdict and commit is not captured. This limit is declared, not simulated.
* **Hosting.** No operated membrane host exists. Evidence-ledger anchors are laboratory anchors, labelled "laboratory anchor, not production".
* **`ESCALATED`.** Remains absorbing by decision in this harness until an authorization model defines who signs, under what scope, and with which receipt. A time-based exit is rejected: expiry produces `UNKNOWN`, not a resolution.

### 10.2 Threats to validity

* **Observation oracle.** In the lab, the membrane is the sole caller of the effect sink, so it observes effects directly; the loss is on the client side. Where the membrane-to-target hop is itself lossy, $O$ does not hold and §5 reduces to Proposition 2.
* **Ground truth in the exploration.** World effects are counted by the harness. This models $O$ and $A$ violations explicitly, but the harness embodies our model of failure.
* **Bounded depth and alphabet.** §6.3.
* **Same-author tests.** §7.5.
* **Fault alphabet and client policy.** The 53 sequences depend on one retry policy and an unweighted enumeration.
* **Concurrency boundary.** S8 tests one targeted two-thread admission race. General safety under arbitrary interleavings, a distributed lock, and crash recovery of membrane state remain unestablished.
* **Trust anchor.** Signature verification is implemented (S7), but the public key is supplied by the caller. The membrane verifies the signature, not the legitimacy of the key. Key resolution via a versioned trust root is tracked separately (D11, issue #14).
* **Separate conformance suite.** The `reg-conformance` suite is a different artifact from the membrane evaluated here. Its published status at the time of our last review recorded failing and uncovered cases and a not-certifiable verdict; we did not re-run it for this paper. Nothing here should be read as certification against that suite.
* **Prior version.** Version 6.6.1 attributed the membrane to `reg-conformance`; the membrane evaluated here is in `tenir-conformance-s1-g1`.
* **Harness drift.** Experiment harnesses can fall behind membrane hardening without failing: the archived current E2 artifact passed CI while exercising no dispatch (§6.2), and the E1 script needed signed evidence to run against the current membrane (§8.2). The replay counts exceptions by type. Two CI checks would have caught it at the first run: the adversarial world must still produce its expected $I_1$ violation, otherwise the run is void; and a run fails when an event class never returns normally.

### 10.3 Not established

The Hypothesis Lab is non-blocking by design. A dedicated anomaly gate fails only when a verdict diverges from its recorded expectation, that is, an expected `FAIL` that becomes `PASS`. An expected `FAIL` is a result, not a failure.

Safety under unconstrained asynchronous concurrency; correctness of a distributed lock; legitimacy of caller-supplied public keys and truthfulness of external-world attestations; correctness of business-unit normalization converters (mixed profiles are rejected, converters are not proven); comparative effectiveness against deployed systems; any property of the scalar kernel.

## 11. Conclusion

We framed post-admission uncertainty as an execution-boundary problem and presented a conditional preservation argument for at-most-once effect under explicit transition obligations and epistemic assumptions.

In the 53-trace E1 comparison, M1 produced no duplicate effects and achieved $15/15$ correct no-effect outcomes and $38/38$ correct single-effect outcomes under the modeled reconciliation assumptions. B2 also prevented duplicates, but achieved $13/38$ correct single-effect outcomes under the stricter client-confirmation metric. M0 prevented duplicates without reconciliation but incurred 96 blocked retry attempts across 51 sequences, illustrating the availability cost of unresolved execution uncertainty.

These results concern the specified model, targeted test cases, and bounded fault policy. They do not establish an unconditional end-to-end guarantee, general correctness under concurrency, or production readiness. The claims concern the membrane layer under the declared assumptions; evaluation of the PVKS diagnostic, the full Gateway decision function, and the scoring kernel remains separate work.

The remaining engineering priorities are to keep experiment harnesses aligned with membrane hardening, define an authorized exit from non-terminal `ESCALATED` states in the conformance harness, bind production attestations to independently verifiable evidence, and extend the exploration to concurrent callers and crash-recovery paths.
