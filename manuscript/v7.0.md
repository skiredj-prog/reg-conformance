# Governing the Unobserved: A Fail-Closed Membrane for Cross-Realm Execution under Post-Admission Epistemic Uncertainty

**Author:** Abdelaziz Skiredj (independent researcher)
**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Version:** 7.0
**Status:** Working paper — theoretical extension with bounded empirical evidence
**Evidence scope:** Formal model; bounded exhaustive exploration of an executable membrane; scripted scenarios S1–S6 (sequential interleavings only); exhaustive comparison of 53 fault sequences against two baselines. No production claim.

---

## Abstract

Distributed operations become difficult to govern when an action may have produced an external effect but its receipt or observation is unavailable. A retry based solely on silence can therefore duplicate an effect. We examine a governance framework organized around the Autonomous Unit (TAU), the decision-making unit capable of acting, and distinguish the functional at-most-once-effect objective from the technical invariants used to refine it at the membrane boundary.

The analysis states four assumptions, A1, A2, O, and A, as defined in §2.3. O denotes observation completeness, while A denotes the truthfulness of the relevant non-execution attestations. The refinement argument is conditional: invariants I2–I8 can support I1 only when the transition rules bind effect-producing dispatches to single-use, attempt-specific permits, prevent unrecorded dispatch paths, preserve terminal attempt records, and require sufficient evidence of non-execution before a subsequent attempt is admitted.

We distinguish three epistemic boundaries: E1, the ambiguity of silence; E2, reliance on truthful attestations; and E3, the concealment limitation in which an externally effective outcome remains unobserved and a false non-execution attestation permits a subsequent attempt. E3 is a specific boundary condition, not a synonym for the general failure of A.

The empirical analysis evaluates 53 fault sequences in E1 and conducts bounded explicit-state exploration in E2 across four assumption regimes. The adversarial exploration finds a three-transition counterexample to I1 within the modeled system. The bounded honest, false-attestation-only, and hidden-but-honest explorations report no I1 violation within their respective limits. A forensic comparison of historical and strict state-key representations changes the number of visited states and recorded violation occurrences but preserves the minimum counterexample depth and the number of distinct minimum-length traces. These results characterize the specified model within the explored bounds; they do not establish an unconditional end-to-end guarantee or prove the correctness of every production implementation.

**Keywords:** cross-Realm governance; execution safety; epistemic uncertainty; fail-closed; reconciliation; fault injection; bounded model checking.

## 1. Introduction

Autonomous agents now request actions whose effects materialize in systems they do not control. Between "the action was authorized" and "the outcome is known" lies an interval in which the requester can be wrong about the world. The standard remedy, retrying until an acknowledgement arrives, is safe only if the target deduplicates. When it does not, a lost acknowledgement turns a single decision into two effects.

This paper isolates that interval. We ask a narrow question: given an admitted transition whose outcome the client cannot observe, what must a boundary component guarantee, under which assumptions, and at what cost?

The scope is deliberately small. We do not address model alignment, intra-Realm correctness or the quality of the admissibility score. The score is an input from a reference kernel (§3.2); the contribution is the lifecycle discipline around it.

**Contributions.**

- **Model (§4).** A transition system with explicit guards and eight invariants, written so that every guard corresponds to a named code path.
- **Conditional safety result (§5).** A conditional refinement theorem and a bounded counterexample illustrating how the at-most-once-effect property can fail under joint violation of the observational and attestation assumptions.
- **Bounded exhaustive verification (§6).** Explicit-state exploration of the executable membrane over its public API, under a 2×2 matrix of assumption violations.
- **Scenario campaign (§7).** Six scripted scenarios, S1–S6, with the evidence artifacts the CI produces.
- **Baseline comparison (§8).** An exhaustive enumeration of fault sequences, reported with its costs.
- **Disclosure of limits (§10).** Including implementation seams found and resolved in the code.

**Non-claims.** This paper does not claim safety under concurrency, a distributed lock, signature verification of evidence, conformance of any production gateway, or that the approach outperforms alternatives in deployed settings.

### 1.1 Scope of evaluation

This paper evaluates the membrane layer of the TENIR architecture: the component that governs the lifecycle of execution attempts after they have been admitted, and that produces the dispositions PASS, HOLD, QUARANTINE_LOCK, and FAILED at the execution boundary.

The paper does not evaluate the following architectural components declared in companion work:

- **PVKS (diagnostic context).** The membrane receives a context vector from PVKS, but the vector is not varied in the experiments. Whether PVKS correctly diagnoses system state is not tested here.
- **The Gateway decision function D(τ, Ω).** The seven dimensions of the admissibility envelope (authority, standing, structural properties, constraint geometry, temporal validity, context) are not exercised. Only their outcome — an admitted attempt — is used as input to the membrane.
- **The scoring kernel (RFC-2).** The reference PolicyEngine is called through a bridge, but the scalar score and its thresholds are not the subject of this paper. All claims below concern the membrane.
- **Cross-Realm transition.** The experiments use one logical operation in one administrative scope. The R_A → R_B transition is the motivating frame, not the tested object.
- **EVP.** Downstream value measurement is not treated.

The reader should treat the results as characterising the membrane layer under the declared assumptions and fault model. Claims about the integrated architecture require separate evaluation of the components above.

## 2. Problem Statement, Fault Model and Related Work

### 2.1 Setting

A Realm is an administrative and technical domain with its own constraint geometry. A transition (\(\tau \colon R_A \to R_B\)) is an action whose effect materializes in (\(R_B\)). A logical operation is identified by a key (\(\ell\)) (called LEI in the implementation). A client makes one or more attempts (\(a\)) to perform the operation; each attempt has a unique identifier.

Local admissibility does not entail cross-Realm admissibility:

\[
\operatorname{Admissible}(a \mid R_A) \nRightarrow \operatorname{Admissible}(\tau \colon R_A \to R_B)
\]

This is the motivation for adjudicating the transition. The present paper concerns what happens after admission.

### 2.2 Fault model

We consider omission and delay faults on the response path and on the evidence path:

- loss of a request before the target (S1a);
- loss of an acknowledgement after the effect (S1b);
- loss of a rejection (S1c);
- no qualifying receipt within a bound (\(\tau_K\)) (S2);
- a second attempt while the first is unresolved (S3);
- a receipt arriving after the window closed or after its token expired (S4);
- bound evidence items that disagree (S5);
- later evidence that settles an unresolved attempt (S6).

Out of scope: Byzantine targets, forged evidence, clock skew between processes, and concurrent callers.

### 2.3 Assumptions

- **A1 (sequential).** Calls to the membrane are totally ordered.
- **A2 (one dispatch).** An attempt dispatches at most once and a dispatch produces at most one external effect.
- **O (observation).** If a dispatch produces an external effect, the membrane records it (`effect_observed`).
- **A (attestation).** An upstream attestation that an operation did not execute is true. E2 corresponds to assumption A below (E2 is defined in §4.0).

A1 and A2 are structural in the implementation. O and A are the two epistemic assumptions whose roles §5 separates.

### 2.4 Related work

**Reference monitors and PEP/PDP.** Anderson's reference monitor concept and XACML-style PEP/PDP architectures evaluate requests against policy at the time of the request [1, 8]. Schneider's characterization of enforceable policies concerns safety properties that a monitor can enforce from observed execution [2]; our setting adds the case in which part of the execution is unobservable to the requester.

**Retries, idempotency and sagas.** Idempotency keys let a target absorb duplicates; sagas compensate partial progress [3]; Helland argues for designing around at-least-once delivery [4]. These techniques are effective when the target cooperates. §8 shows that a PEP/PDP with a reused idempotency key achieves zero duplicates on our metric, and positions the membrane relative to it.

**Impossibility of common knowledge over lossy links.** The two-generals problem [5] explains why no protocol over a lossy channel can give both parties certainty about the other's state. The membrane does not escape this; it chooses what to do while uncertain (hold), and when uncertainty may be resolved (only on bound, fresh, qualified evidence).

**Formal methods.** Explicit-state exploration of a specification [6, 7] is standard. Our exploration applies to the executable implementation through its public API rather than to a separate specification.

**Companion work.** The REG Core Protocol working paper (record: https://zenodo.org/records/23252697, version v0.3.0 v3) addresses the protocol layer; this manuscript does not rely on its content beyond the shared vocabulary. The membrane evaluated here is in `tenir-conformance-s1-g1`; the scoring kernel is `tenir_governance`.

## 4. Governance Framework and the Autonomous Unit (TAU)

This section establishes the functional governance contract for a logical operation and identifies the Autonomous Unit (TAU) as the decision-making unit capable of acting, consistent with Blueprint v4.4 §0.1. It distinguishes that functional contract from the technical invariants governing the membrane's local state transitions.

The TAU operates within a declared scope and under the applicable governance constraints, evidence requirements, and authorized resolution mechanisms. It does not possess unlimited authority over external systems, determine external truth by declaration, or guarantee that every real-world effect is observable. In particular, the absence of a receipt is not, by itself, evidence that an operation did not execute.

Throughout this section and the remainder of the manuscript, **TAU means Autonomous Unit**. The expressions "Tenant Authority Unit," "Threshold of Autonomous Unity," and "Temporal Admissibility Unit" are not used.

The governance contract distinguishes three outcomes relevant to execution control. `HOLD` is a disposition that withholds admission pending sufficient evidence or review. `QUARANTINE_LOCK` is a persistent LEI-level guard that blocks new admission and dispatch until an authorized resolution. Neither term changes the truth of external events, and neither permits the membrane to infer non-execution from silence.

### 4.0 Functional Declaration: The Autonomous Unit (TAU)

#### 1. Functional definition and scope

The Autonomous Unit (TAU) is the decision-making unit capable of acting, as defined in Blueprint v4.4 §0.1. For the purposes of this manuscript, it governs the admission and lifecycle of attempts associated with a logical operation identified by a Logical Execution Identifier (LEI), within a declared governance scope.

The TAU may admit an attempt, refuse admission, place an operation on `HOLD`, or engage a `QUARANTINE_LOCK`, subject to the applicable contract and authorization constraints. These powers are bounded: the TAU governs the membrane's admissibility decisions but does not control external-world truth, make an unobserved effect disappear, or independently establish that a remote operation did not execute.

Attempt-level state and LEI-level governance state are distinct. An attempt may reach a terminal status such as `RESOLVED` or `FAILED`. A `QUARANTINE_LOCK` applies at the LEI level and prevents new execution admission; it does not rewrite the terminal status of an existing attempt.

#### 2. Preservation objective: safety takes priority over liveness

The functional safety objective is **at-most-once effect**. For each LEI \(L\) and every finite execution-history prefix \(\pi\), let \(E_L(\pi)\) denote the number of externally effective outcomes attributable to \(L\) in that prefix. The invariant is:

$$
I_1(\pi): \quad |E_L(\pi)| \leq 1
$$

The property concerns real external effects, not merely effects recorded in the membrane's local state. Counting an effect only when it is observed would weaken the property and would fail to detect a concealed outcome.

Safety takes priority over liveness. If the evidence does not establish that a further attempt is safe under the declared assumptions and transition rules, the system must withhold admission. A timeout, network partition, missing receipt, or unacknowledged dispatch does not by itself establish non-execution.

The at-most-once property is a safety property: a violation can be demonstrated by a finite execution prefix containing more than one externally effective outcome for the same LEI. The property does not itself guarantee that an operation eventually completes.

#### 3. Epistemic boundaries: E1, E2, and E3

The TAU receives information about external execution through evidence available to the membrane. Cryptographic integrity and source authentication can support provenance and tamper detection; they do not, by themselves, establish the physical truth of an attestation.

**E1 — Ambiguity of silence.** A timeout, network partition, lost response, or unacknowledged dispatch leaves execution status unknown unless separate qualifying evidence establishes the outcome. Silence must not be treated as proof of non-execution. It cannot, by itself, justify `FAILED` or the admission of a subsequent attempt.

**E2 — Attestation reliance.** The reasoning that relies on a non-execution attestation depends on assumption A in §2.3: the relevant attestation is truthful. A cryptographically authenticated statement can still be false about the external world. Authentication and integrity do not entail truthfulness.

**E3 — The concealment limitation.** E3 names a specific limitation of the end-to-end safety argument. Conceptually, the threat involves an authenticated but false non-execution attestation; however, the empirical model tests failure of attestation truthfulness without verifying cryptographic signatures. A witness to this limitation in the model requires the following conditions:

1. An attempt produces a real external effect that the membrane does not observe or register before a later admission decision.
2. A false non-execution attestation (modeled as a qualified declaration) permits that attempt to be declared `FAILED`, or otherwise causes the admission guard to accept a subsequent attempt.
3. The subsequent attempt is dispatched and produces another real effect for the same LEI.

This sequence can violate I1. The general failure of assumption A, denoted \(\neg A\), is not identical to E3. A false attestation does not necessarily produce an I1 violation if observation or another effective guard prevents the subsequent dispatch. E3 identifies the specific concealment conditions under which the safety argument fails.

#### 4. Admissibility and failure behavior

**Evidentiary prerequisite.** A subsequent attempt \(A_n\) may be admitted only if every prior attempt associated with the LEI satisfies the applicable terminal-state and evidence requirements. A recorded effect count of zero is not sufficient when it counts only observed effects and observation completeness is unavailable.

**Contradictory or unbound evidence.** Contradictory evidence, expired or improperly bound evidence, and evidence that fails the applicable integrity or authority checks must not be resolved through optimistic inference. Where the conflict prevents a safe admission decision, execution remains blocked pending qualifying evidence or authorized resolution.

**HOLD.** `HOLD` withholds admission for the decision at issue while the evidence or governance question remains unresolved. It is a disposition, not automatically a persistent LEI-level quarantine. A later decision must be based on admissible evidence and the applicable transition rules.

**QUARANTINE_LOCK.** `QUARANTINE_LOCK` blocks new attempt admission and execution dispatch for the LEI until an authorized release or resolution transition succeeds. It does not erase or reopen terminal attempt records. Read-only inspection, append-only evidence recording, and explicitly authorized reconciliation may remain available, provided those transitions cannot dispatch an operation or bypass the lock. Release must be authenticated and authorized under the applicable governance contract.

When the system cannot establish that a subsequent execution is safe, it must withhold admission. Whether the appropriate disposition is `HOLD` or `QUARANTINE_LOCK` depends on the declared transition and escalation rules; the two states must not be treated as interchangeable.

### 4.1 State

For each attempt (\(a\)) on operation (\(\ell\)):

- (\(\sigma(a) \in \{\mathrm{PENDING},\ \mathrm{AWAITING},\ \mathrm{UNKNOWN},\ \mathrm{FAILED},\ \mathrm{RESOLVED},\ \mathrm{ESCALATED}\}\));
- (\(e(a) \in \{0,1\}\)): the membrane has recorded an external effect for (\(a\));
- (\(q(a) \in \{0,1\}\)): the outcome is qualified;
- a permit (\(\pi(a) \in \{\mathrm{issued},\ \mathrm{consumed},\ \mathrm{revoked}\}\)) and a nonce.

Globally: a quarantine set (\(Q\)) of operations, an evidence registry, and the effect sink.

Let (\(w(a)\)) denote the world effect count of (\(a\)), the number of external effects actually produced by (\(a\)). The membrane cannot read (\(w\)); it records (\(e\)). Assumption O states (\(w(a) > 0 \Rightarrow e(a) = 1\)).

### 4.2 Client projection

The client sees only (\(\rho(a) \in \{\mathrm{PASS},\ \mathrm{HOLD},\ \mathrm{HARD\_VETO}\} \times \text{state label}\)). In the implementation, S1a, S1b and S1c all project to (\((\mathrm{HOLD},\ \mathrm{UNKNOWN})\)), although (\(w(a)\)) is 0, 1 and 0. This is client-side projection indiscernibility. The membrane's internal (\(e(a)\)) differs across the three cases; the client's view does not.

### 4.3 Admission guard

A new attempt (\(a'\)) on (\(\ell\)) is admitted iff

$$
G_{\mathrm{adm}}(\ell, a') \equiv a' \notin \mathrm{dom}(\sigma) \wedge \ell \notin Q \wedge \forall a \text{ on } \ell: \sigma(a) = \mathrm{FAILED} \wedge \operatorname{NonExecutionEstablished}(a)
$$

where \(\operatorname{NonExecutionEstablished}(a)\) requires that the attempt reached `FAILED` through a transition supported by admissible evidence of non-execution (e.g., an observed rejection or a qualified attestation under assumption A), rather than merely \(e(a) = 0\).

On admission a permit is issued. If the kernel vetoes, the permit is not consumed. Otherwise it is consumed at dispatch.

### 4.5 Preamble

To connect the functional preservation objective declared by the Autonomous Unit with the membrane's operational state machine, we specify the following technical invariants. These invariants constrain local transitions; their ability to establish a property about real external effects additionally depends on the environmental assumptions and on the precise definition of effect-producing transitions.

The derivation below distinguishes an invariant itself from the transition conditions required to use that invariant in a proof. In particular, the absence of an observed effect is not equivalent to the absence of a real effect when observation completeness does not hold.

### 4.5a At-Most-Once Effect and Supporting Invariants I1–I8

#### Scope and notation

Let \(\pi\) denote a finite execution-history prefix of the modeled transition system, and let \(E_L(\pi)\) be the number of real external effects for LEI \(L\) along that prefix. I1 is a property of every valid execution-history prefix, not merely of a final snapshot:

$$
I_1(\pi): \quad |E_L(\pi)| \leq 1
$$

The invariants below describe the obligations required to preserve I1. They do not establish the theorem merely by being listed. The proof also requires that the transition relation implement the stated dispatch, evidence, and admission conditions.

| Invariant | Formal rule / property | Role in the preservation argument and required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I1 — At-most-once effect** | \(\|E_L(\pi)\| \leq 1\) for every valid finite execution-history prefix \(\pi\) | The functional safety property to be preserved. The counter counts real external effects, not only effects observed or recorded locally. |
| **I2 — Permit single-use** | A permit bound to an attempt and LEI may authorize at most one effect-producing dispatch and cannot be consumed again. | Prevents replay of the same permit. The transition rules must ensure that every effect-producing dispatch requires the appropriate attempt-bound permit and that the permit cannot authorize repeated dispatches. The invariant does not mean merely that permits are not used concurrently. |
| **I3 — Attempt terminality and immutability** | Once an attempt reaches `RESOLVED` or `FAILED`, its terminal status cannot be changed or reopened. | Prevents retroactive rewriting of an attempt's outcome. This is distinct from the LEI-level `QUARANTINE_LOCK`. Append-only evidence and reconciliation records may be permitted, but they must not silently rewrite terminal attempt status or re-enable dispatch. |
| **I4 — Only dispatch produces effects** | A real effect can be produced only by a valid effect-producing dispatch under the modeled transition rules. Each such dispatch produces at most one real effect for the modeled operation. | Excludes effect-producing transitions that bypass dispatch. The at-most-one-effect-per-dispatch condition must be explicit in the model; it does not follow merely from naming a transition "dispatch." |
| **I5 — FAILED implies no observed effect** | \(\operatorname{State}(A_k)=\texttt{FAILED}\Rightarrow e_{\mathrm{obs}}(A_k)=0\) | This is a statement about observed effects. By itself it does not establish that the real effect count is zero. Under O, a real effect cannot remain unobserved within the scope of O. Alternatively, a truthful non-execution attestation under A can establish non-execution for the attempt it covers. The transition rules must specify which admissible evidence supports `FAILED`. |
| **I6 — No admission under quarantine** | \(\operatorname{LEIState}(L)=\texttt{QUARANTINE\_LOCK}\Rightarrow\text{no new attempt admission or dispatch for }L\) | Blocks attempt creation for execution, permit-based authorization of a new attempt, and effect-producing dispatch while the lock is active. Read-only inspection, append-only evidence updates, permitted reconciliation, and authorized release may remain available. It does not claim that every state mutation is prohibited. |
| **I7 — Admission condition** | \(\operatorname{Admit}(A_n)\Rightarrow\forall k<n:\operatorname{State}(A_k)=\texttt{FAILED}\land\operatorname{NonExecutionEstablished}(A_k)\) | Requires every prior attempt to be terminally failed and requires evidence sufficient to establish non-execution under the applicable assumptions. A zero count of observed effects alone is not sufficient if O is absent and no truthful attestation under A establishes non-execution. |
| **I8 — Attempt-history permanence** | For valid prefixes \(\pi_1\preceq\pi_2\), the attempt records in \(\pi_1\) remain present and unaltered in \(\pi_2\). | Prevents deletion, reuse, or retroactive rewriting of attempt history. New evidence may be appended under the evidence rules, but existing attempt records cannot be pruned to bypass I7. |

Here, \(e_{\mathrm{obs}}\) denotes the observed effect count for an attempt. \(\operatorname{NonExecutionEstablished}\) denotes a decision justified by qualifying evidence under the assumptions applicable to that path; it is not defined merely by \(e_{\mathrm{obs}}=0\).

**Transition-level proof obligations.** To use I2–I8 to establish I1, the modeled transition relation must additionally satisfy all of the following:

1. The initial state has no real effects for the LEI.
2. Every transition that increases the real effect count is an effect-producing dispatch covered by I4.
3. Each attempt's effect-producing dispatch is protected by its unique single-use permit under I2, with no alternative dispatch path.
4. Under O, every real effect in scope is represented in the observation/evidence state before a subsequent admission decision can rely on its absence.
5. A transition to `FAILED` cannot be based solely on timeout, silence, or missing observation. It requires evidence admissible under the declared contract.
6. Under A, an attestation of non-execution used for admission is truthful for the attempt and operation it covers.
7. I7 checks all prior attempts, and I8 prevents prior attempts from being omitted or rewritten. I6 blocks subsequent admission while the LEI-level quarantine lock is active.

Assumptions A1 and A2 remain exactly as defined in §2.3. Their required role in the transition relation must be stated where used; the invariant labels alone do not establish their operational satisfaction.

## 5. Preamble

The formal relationship between the functional preservation objective and the technical invariants is expressed as a conditional refinement claim. The claim concerns the real external effect count across all finite execution-history prefixes of the modeled transition system. Its validity depends on the transition-level obligations in §4.5a and on the applicable observational and attestation assumptions, O and A, as defined in §2.3.

The following result must therefore be read as a conditional safety argument for the specified model, not as an unconditional guarantee for arbitrary external systems or for an implementation whose transitions have not been shown to refine that model.

### 5a Refinement Statement: Conditional Guarantee of the Functional Contract

#### 1. Refinement claim

**Theorem 1 — Conditional preservation of at-most-once effect.**

Let \(S_0\) be the initial state of the modeled membrane transition system. Let \(\pi\) be any finite execution-history prefix reachable from \(S_0\), and let \(E_L(\pi)\) denote the real external effects attributable to LEI \(L\) in that prefix.

If the following conditions hold:

1. the initial state contains zero real external effects for \(L\);
2. invariants I2–I8 hold on every transition along \(\pi\);
3. every real effect is produced only by a valid effect-producing dispatch, and each such dispatch produces at most one real effect for its attempt;
4. every effect-producing dispatch requires the unique single-use permit bound to that attempt and LEI;
5. O holds for the effect-observation scope used by the admission decision, or the relevant non-execution conclusion is independently established by a truthful attestation under A;
6. every prior attempt must satisfy I7 before a subsequent attempt can be admitted, with `FAILED` supported by admissible evidence of non-execution rather than silence alone;
7. no transition outside the specified dispatch rules can increase the real external effect count;

then:

$$
|E_L(\pi)| \leq 1
$$

for every such reachable finite execution-history prefix \(\pi\).

This is a conditional theorem. Its hypotheses must be checked against the actual transition relation. A successful bounded exploration is evidence about the explored model and its tested implementation; it does not discharge the general proof obligations by itself.

#### 2. Proof structure

**Base case.** In the initial state \(S_0\), the real external effect count for \(L\) is zero by condition 1. Therefore \(|E_L(\pi_0)|=0\leq1\).

**Inductive hypothesis.** Assume that the property holds for a reachable prefix \(\pi\): \(|E_L(\pi)|\leq1\).

**Inductive step.** Consider a valid transition \(t\) extending \(\pi\) to \(\pi'\).

- If \(t\) is not an effect-producing dispatch, condition 7 ensures that it does not increase the real effect count. Therefore the bound is preserved.
- If \(t\) is an effect-producing dispatch and the prior real effect count is zero, condition 3 permits at most one new real effect. Thus the count in \(\pi'\) is at most one.
- If a real effect has already occurred, a further effect-producing dispatch must be prevented. I2 prevents replay of the consumed permit for the same attempt. I7 prevents a subsequent attempt from being admitted until the preceding attempts satisfy the terminal and non-execution evidence requirements. Under O, an earlier real effect cannot remain unobserved within the scope of the admission decision. Under A, a non-execution attestation used to qualify an earlier attempt as `FAILED` cannot truthfully certify non-execution if that attempt produced the effect it covers. Thus, subject to conditions 3–6, a previously effect-producing attempt cannot be used to justify a new attempt on the basis of false or insufficient non-execution evidence.

Consequently, no valid transition increases the real effect count from one to two. The property is preserved at \(\pi'\). By induction over finite execution-history prefixes, I1 holds for every reachable prefix satisfying the stated conditions.

**Proof limitation.** This proof is valid only if the listed conditions are enforced by the actual transition relation. In particular, I5 in its observed-effect form is not sufficient on its own to establish the absence of a real effect, and I2–I8 without the dispatch and evidence obligations do not automatically imply I1.

#### 3. Joint role of I2–I8

The supporting invariants address different ways a later effect could be produced:

- **I2** prevents reuse of a consumed attempt-bound permit.
- **I3** prevents terminal attempt outcomes from being rewritten or reopened. It does not turn `QUARANTINE_LOCK` into an attempt status.
- **I4** restricts effect production to dispatch transitions and must explicitly bound the number of effects produced by each dispatch.
- **I5** constrains observed state. The implication from no observed effect to no real effect requires O or an applicable truthful non-execution attestation under A.
- **I6** blocks admission and dispatch while the LEI is under `QUARANTINE_LOCK`; evidence collection and authorized reconciliation may still proceed under their own transition rules.
- **I7** requires all earlier attempts to be terminally failed with non-execution established by admissible evidence before a subsequent attempt is admitted.
- **I8** prevents deletion or retroactive rewriting of the history on which I7 relies.

These invariants form a joint preservation argument only under the conditions above. The wording "I2–I8 imply I1" must not be interpreted as an unconditional logical entailment independent of the transition relation or environmental assumptions.

#### 4. Partial refinement when assumptions do not all hold

**Proposition 2 — Conditional guarantee when O is absent but A holds.**

Suppose O does not hold. I1 may nevertheless be preserved along execution histories satisfying the following path restriction: every transition to `FAILED` that is used to admit a subsequent attempt is supported by a truthful non-execution attestation under A; the attestation covers the relevant attempt and operation; every earlier attempt is checked by I7; and I2–I4, I6, I8, and the applicable transition obligations remain in force.

Under these conditions, the truthful non-execution evidence can establish that the attempts admitted for retry produced no real effect, even though observation completeness is absent. The preservation argument applies only to paths satisfying the restriction.

A path that reaches `FAILED` on the basis of an unobserved rejection, timeout, lost receipt, or other condition that does not establish non-execution is outside this proposition. Where O is absent and no truthful, qualifying attestation establishes non-execution, I7 cannot safely admit a new attempt merely because the observed effect count is zero.

**Proposition 3 — Model-specific counterexample under the concealment boundary.**

The absence of O and A does not logically force every possible system to violate I1. However, the current bounded E2 adversarial model contains a reachable finite trace that violates I1:

1. `admit_await(A1,HIDDEN_effect)`
2. `declare_failed(A1,qualified=True)`
3. `process(A2,ok)`

In the modeled trace, the first attempt produces a real effect not observed by the membrane. The subsequent qualified failure is accepted without a truthful non-execution basis, and the second attempt produces another real effect for the same LEI. The model's real-world counter reaches two, violating I1.

This trace is a counterexample to the universal I1 claim for the modeled transition system if the listed transitions are admitted under the stated adversarial conditions. The bounded explorer found this three-transition witness; that empirical result does not establish that every environment violating O or A exhibits the same trace, nor does it demonstrate that all real implementations admit the same transitions.

The trace illustrates E3 because it combines a hidden first effect, a false non-execution attestation, and a subsequent effect-producing dispatch. The experiment models failure of attestation truthfulness; it does not test cryptographic authentication, signature verification, or adversarial signature forgery. The bounded `false_attest` world, in which O holds but A is violated, reported no I1 violation within depth 6; that finding is bounded evidence, not a universal guarantee.

#### 5. Boundary statement

The Autonomous Unit declares the functional governance contract in §4.0. The technical invariants in §4.5a define the local preservation obligations. The refinement claim holds only where the stated transition and environmental conditions hold.

Under E3, the specified concealment trace demonstrates that local admissibility decisions can fail to preserve the end-to-end at-most-once-effect objective when a real effect is hidden and false non-execution evidence permits a retry. The architecture therefore states this boundary explicitly instead of presenting the conditional result as an unconditional safety guarantee.

> "The functional declaration holds where the stated assumptions and transition obligations hold. Where the evidence cannot establish non-execution, or where the environment can conceal an effect while supplying false non-execution evidence, the architecture declares the boundary rather than claiming a guarantee it cannot support."

## 7. Scenario Campaign S1–S6

### 7.1 Scenarios and properties

| ID | Situation | Property tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Lost request, lost acknowledgement, lost rejection | Uncertainty does not authorize re-execution |
| S2 | No qualifying evidence by \(\tau_K\) | Expiry yields UNKNOWN/HOLD, not success or failure |
| S3 | Retry while an earlier attempt is unresolved | The operation lock blocks a second admission; attempt_id is globally unique |
| S4 | Receipt after window closure or token expiry | Late proof does not reopen the right to execute |
| S5 | Contradictory evidence | The conflict is preserved and escalated; no optimistic resolution |
| S6 | Reliable evidence settles an unresolved attempt | Convergence without re-execution or permit reuse |

### 7.2 Results

All 68 membrane tests and the 2 real-kernel probe tests pass in a local run (70 total). The post-merge CI run recorded in the repository manifest (run 56, commit `84e46d94a6259ad1ec57757074a422d2f356ec49`) passed and published evidence archives. Per-file counts: S1 harness 6, S1 with real kernel 5, S2 12, S3 6, S4 10, S5 11, S6 7, T7/T8 11.

Each scenario emits a transition log, a permit registry snapshot, an effect-sink snapshot, a summary and the SHA-256 of its definition.

### 7.3 S4 and S6 are different mechanisms

S4 rejects a receipt that attempts to qualify an execution after the window closed. S6 uses a separate `reconcile` operation to observe a past outcome. If reconciliation reused the late-receipt path it would become a bypass of S4. The implementation keeps them apart: `reconcile` does not reopen the window, does not call the sink, does not issue permits and does not reuse the first attempt's permit.

### 7.4 The attestation limit

`non_execution_confirmed` is a declarative upstream attestation. The membrane checks that it is present and boolean; it does not verify a signature. A production deployment must bind it to a signed target receipt or an independent audit path. Together with O, this is the assumption on which §5 rests.

### 7.5 What the campaign does not show

The scenarios are scripted and sequential. They were written by the same team that wrote the membrane. They demonstrate that the specified behaviour exists for the specified cases; §6 reduces, but does not remove, the dependence on case selection.

## 8. Comparison with Baselines

### 8.1 Design

A single logical operation, a fault alphabet per attempt (F0 success; F1 request lost; F2 effect applied, acknowledgement lost; F3 rejection lost; F4 rejection observed), and a client that retries after a no-response outcome (F1–F3), up to three attempts. All sequences consistent with this policy are enumerated: 53 sequences. This is an enumeration, not a probability estimate; sequences are unweighted.

Systems:

- **B1** stateless PEP/PDP: kernel decision, then dispatch;
- **B2** B1 with a target-side idempotency key reused across retries;
- **M0** the membrane without any reconciliation;
- **M1** the membrane with evidence-bound reconciliation using truthful attestations between a no-response outcome and the next retry.

### 8.2 Results

The results below retain the original outcome counts and add two trace-conditioned metrics. The 53 enumerated sequences divide into 15 sequences in which no effect is expected and 38 in which an effect is expected.

A **correct no-effect outcome** requires both zero final effects and positive operation-level evidence establishing non-execution. An unresolved `UNKNOWN` or `HOLD` state alone does not qualify.

A **correct single-effect outcome** requires exactly one final effect and client-confirmed success. The denominators are determined from the enumerated fault traces, not from the system's outputs.

| System | Duplicate effects | Exactly one effect | No effect | Client-confirmed success | Correct no-effect | Correct single-effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 — Stateless PEP/PDP | 14 | 24 | 15 | 13 | 1/15 (6.67%) | 7/38 (18.42%) |
| B2 — PEP/PDP + reused idempotency key | 0 | 38 | 15 | 13 | 1/15 (6.67%) | 13/38 (34.21%) |
| M0 — Membrane without reconciliation | 0 | 18 | 35 | 1 | 1/15 (6.67%) | 1/38 (2.63%) |
| M1 — Membrane with evidence-bound reconciliation | 0 | 38 | 15 | 38 | 15/15 (100%) | 38/38 (100%) |

The correct no-effect metric is deliberately stricter than simply counting sequences with zero effects. It measures whether non-execution is established for the logical operation, rather than merely remaining possible.

These are finite, unweighted model results. They are not estimates of real-world probabilities and do not establish production performance.

### 8.3 Interpretation

**Duplicates.** B1 produces 14 duplicate-effect sequences. B2 and the membrane produce none under the specified model. A reused target-side idempotency key is sufficient to prevent duplicates on this metric; the membrane's contribution is not that idempotency is ineffective, but that execution-lifecycle controls are enforced at the boundary without relying on target-side deduplication.

**Correct single-effect outcomes.** B1 achieves 7 correct single-effect outcomes among 38 effect-expected traces. B2 achieves 13/38: the reused idempotency key prevents duplicate effects, but does not establish success to the client whenever a receipt is lost. M0 achieves 1/38 because fail-closed admission blocks later attempts without a reconciliation path. M1 achieves 38/38 under the modeled assumption that the evidence-bound reconciliation channel is reliable and truthful.

**Correct no-effect outcomes.** For B1, B2 and M0, only 1 of the 15 no-effect-expected traces establishes non-execution for the entire logical operation. The only no-effect trace correctly resolved by B1, B2 and M0 is the single-attempt F4 trace: the target receives the request, explicitly rejects it, and the client observes the rejection. M1 establishes it for all 15 through the modeled reconciliation mechanism. This result depends on the evidence assumptions; it does not establish that an arbitrary upstream attestation is truthful.

**Availability and blocked attempts.** M0 records 96 blocked retry attempts across 51 of the 53 sequences. These are different quantities: 51 is the number of sequences containing at least one block, while 96 is the total number of blocked attempts. The distinction matters because the client continues its bounded retry loop after an admission refusal treated as `HOLD`.

**Client knowledge.** M1's improved outcome is conditional on reliable, truthful reconciliation evidence. The difference between B2 and M1 therefore reflects not just execution deduplication but the ability to establish an operation's outcome. This is a modeled property of the combined evidence and membrane design, not a demonstrated property of the membrane alone in production.

**Baseline scope.** B1 and B2 do not model stale or contradictory evidence (S4 and S5). The comparison is limited to the declared fault alphabet, one logical operation, at most three attempts, and the specified client retry policy. The sequences are exhaustively enumerated under that policy and are not probability-weighted.

## 10. Limitations and Threats to Validity

### 10.1 Seams found in the implementation

Two previously reproduced seams were closed in commit `3ef1d45` (membrane fix). One remains open.

- **Closed — nonce binding and reconciliation entry point.** The `process_transaction` path now requires a non-empty nonce and records it with the attempt, supporting evidence-bound reconciliation through that entry point. Regression tests were added in the same commit.

- **Closed — pre-execution `HARD_VETO` lifecycle.** A kernel veto now places the attempt in `FAILED` with `non_execution_confirmed=True`, records the veto, and unlocks the LEI without consuming an execution permit. This addresses the earlier behavior in which a veto left the attempt unresolved and blocked subsequent legitimate requests.

- **Open — `ESCALATED` remains absorbing.** Following a contradiction on a non-terminal attempt, `reconcile` returns a bad-state response and no authorized exit transition is currently available. Terminal-state quarantine has a release procedure, but the non-terminal `ESCALATED` path does not. This remains an operational and availability limitation.

The closures above apply to the lab artifact and the tested code revision. They do not establish correctness under concurrency, production evidence authentication, or conformance of a deployed gateway.

### 10.2 Threats to validity

- **Observation oracle.** In the lab, the membrane is the sole caller of the effect sink, so it observes effects directly; the loss is on the client side. Where the membrane-to-target hop is itself lossy, O does not hold and §5 reduces to Proposition 2.
- **Ground truth in the exploration.** World effects are counted by the harness. This models O and A violations explicitly, but the harness embodies our model of failure.
- **Bounded depth and alphabet.** §6.3.
- **Same-author tests.** §7.5.
- **Fault alphabet and client policy.** The 53 sequences depend on one retry policy and an unweighted enumeration.
- **No concurrency.** A1. Safety under interleaved calls, a distributed lock, or crash recovery of membrane state is not addressed.
- **No signature verification.** §7.4.
- **Separate conformance suite.** The `reg-conformance` suite is a different artifact from the membrane evaluated here. Its published status at the time of our last review recorded failing and uncovered cases and a not-certifiable verdict; we did not re-run it for this paper. Nothing here should be read as certification against that suite.
- **Prior version.** Version 6.6.1 attributed the membrane to `reg-conformance`; the membrane evaluated here is in `tenir-conformance-s1-g1`.

### 10.3 Not established

Safety under asynchronous concurrency; correctness of a distributed lock; cryptographic verification of evidence; correctness of business-unit normalization converters (mixed profiles are rejected, converters are not proven); comparative effectiveness against deployed systems; any property of the scalar kernel.

## 11. Conclusion

We framed post-admission uncertainty as an execution-boundary problem and presented a conditional refinement argument for at-most-once effect under explicit transition obligations and epistemic assumptions.

In the 53-trace E1 comparison, M1 produced no duplicate effects and achieved 15/15 correct no-effect outcomes and 38/38 correct single-effect outcomes under the modeled reconciliation assumptions. B2 also prevented duplicates, but achieved 13/38 correct single-effect outcomes under the stricter client-confirmation metric. M0 prevented duplicates without reconciliation but incurred 96 blocked retry attempts across 51 sequences, illustrating the availability cost of unresolved execution uncertainty.

These results concern a specified sequential model and bounded fault policy. They do not establish an unconditional end-to-end guarantee, correctness under concurrency, or production readiness. These results concern the membrane layer under the declared assumptions. Evaluation of the PVKS diagnostic, the Gateway decision function, and the scoring kernel remains separate work.

The remaining engineering priorities are to define an authorized exit from non-terminal `ESCALATED` states, bind production attestations to independently verifiable evidence, and extend the exploration to concurrent callers and crash-recovery paths.
